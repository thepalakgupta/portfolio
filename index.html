About Me

I started as a Data Analyst (lots of SQL, lots of dashboards). Somewhere between building models and staring at funnels, I realized the most interesting part wasn't the chart—it was the decision someone made because of it.

So I chased that thread. Through freelance projects, into fintech, through A/B tests and cohorts. Until "data analyst" started feeling less like a title and more like training for something bigger. Started thinking like a PM. Asking "why" before "how." Seeing data as a tool for building products, not reporting metrics.

Now I split focus between three things: keeping the analytical rigor that got me here, building the ML instincts I picked up, and learning to ask product questions that change roadmaps. Still rewrite queries because joins feel inelegant. Still think a good dashboard is just a story told in numbers.
